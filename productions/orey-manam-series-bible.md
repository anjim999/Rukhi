# OREY… MANAM! — SERIES MASTER PROMPT

## PROJECT IDENTITY

Title: OREY… MANAM!
Format: Cinematic 3D animated Indian youth comedy-drama.
Target audience: Young adults, approximately 18–30.
Visual style: Realistic, premium-quality 3D animation with natural human proportions, expressive facial animation, realistic materials, cinematic lighting, and believable environmental details.
Aspect ratio: 16:9.
Frame rate: 24 fps.
Dialogue: Natural Indian English, with subtle regional Telugu conversational influences where appropriate.
Tone: Funny, warm, contemporary, emotionally relatable, and grounded in everyday Indian youth culture.

## SERIES CONCEPT

Tell the story of a close-knit group of young Indian friends navigating friendships, birthdays, money problems, careers, relationships, family expectations, and the transition into adulthood.

The humor must emerge from contrasting personalities, awkward situations, timing, sarcasm, misunderstandings, and authentic friendship dynamics.

Treat the characters as believable young adults, not cartoon stereotypes.

## VISUAL REALISM

Create realistic 3D animated human characters with:
- Natural Indian facial structures and believable human proportions.
- Detailed hair, eyebrows, eyelashes, skin shading, and facial micro-expressions.
- Realistic eye movement, blinking, breathing, posture, and hand gestures.
- Physically believable cloth, fabric folds, furniture, glass, plastic, and smartphone materials.
- Natural daylight, soft shadows, global illumination, reflections, and cinematic depth of field.
- Convincing facial muscles and mouth movements synchronized to speech.
- Subtle imperfections that make environments and characters feel lived-in.

The result must resemble a professionally directed animated streaming series, not a children's cartoon, a mobile game, or a slideshow of still images.

Maintain realism without making the characters look uncannily photorealistic. Favor coherent, attractive character design and natural acting.

Santhosh: warm male voice; medium pitch; relaxed pace; natural conversational delivery.

Use natural conversational pauses, believable laughter, soft breaths, hesitation, overlapping reactions where appropriate, and accurate lip synchronization.

## CINEMATOGRAPHY

Use motivated, professional camera work:
- Wide establishing shots for rooms and environments.
- Medium shots for actions and performances.
- Close-ups for punchlines, facial reactions, and emotional shifts.
- Over-the-shoulder shots for phone interactions.
- Insert shots for phone messages, notifications, hands, and the banking balance.
- Reaction shots that allow comedic pauses to land.
- Slow push-ins for important emotional or comedic beats.
- Rapid cuts during group-chat reactions, while keeping every image visually readable.

Use believable lens perspective and controlled depth of field. A cinematic lens equivalent of 24–35 mm works well for environments, 35–50 mm for medium shots, and 65–85 mm for close-ups.

Do not apply dramatic camera movement to every shot. Most comedy should come from the performances and edits.

Preserve screen direction, eyelines, lighting direction, room geography, and character positions across consecutive shots.

## ENVIRONMENTAL CONTINUITY

Treat each recurring location as a permanent set.

Karthik's bedroom must retain the same room layout, wall color, window, curtains, furniture, bed, pillows, chair, phone, and clock.

Arjun's office meeting room, Meera's workspace, Santhosh's eating area, Rohit's room, and Vicky's client-call setup must each maintain their own consistent set design.

The opening takes place at 10:47 AM. Maintain late-morning daylight and the same scene chronology.

Keep the smartphone, wardrobe, props, and character positions consistent unless the script calls for a change.

## STORY AND PERFORMANCE RULES

Follow the supplied screenplay exactly.

Do not rewrite the dialogue, alter the punchlines, change the bank balance, add new characters, omit reactions, or invent additional plot developments without approval.

Play the humor naturally. Avoid forced comedy, overacting, exaggerated mouth movements, or artificial reaction faces.

Allow silence to work as comedy. Facial expressions and pauses are as important as dialogue.

## AUDIO DESIGN

Dialogue must remain clear and foregrounded.

Use restrained background music, natural room ambience, smartphone vibration, notification sounds, typing, office ambience, and relevant environmental sound effects.

Use music to shape the buildup and punchlines, but do not cover spoken words. Silence is permitted and encouraged for comedic timing.

## DELIVERY STANDARD

Generate coherent, editable cinematic clips with clean starts and endings.

Prefer short, controlled shots over a single long generation containing many camera changes and several speaking characters.

Maintain consistent character identities, saved voices, environmental continuity, and visual style across the entire series.

Prioritize:
1. Character identity consistency.
2. Correct dialogue and speaker assignment.
3. Natural facial animation and lip synchronization.
4. Story continuity.
5. Cinematic visual quality.
6. Comedic timing.

The final series should look and sound like a unified production, not unrelated AI-generated clips.
